# Review 4: government fit

Reviewer stance: a government digital service assessor (GOV.UK service assessments, the DTA Digital Service Standard, GovTech Singapore, and UAE federal and Dubai digital programmes). The question is whether the Shared Channels User Experience Standard 2.0 will work in Dubai government, not whether it is good design.

Document reviewed: `docs/shared-channels-ux-standard.md` (version 2.0, draft, 2,157 lines), read in full. Context read: `docs/v2/plan.md`, `docs/v2/decisions.md` and `design-system/docs/channel-sites-audit.md` (from the `sc-ux-standards` tree, because it is not in this worktree).

Line numbers refer to `docs/shared-channels-ux-standard.md` as it is in this worktree.

---

## Executive verdict

**Strong on service behaviour. Not yet adoptable as a government standard.**

The service behaviour content (chapters 3, 5, 9, 12 and 13) is among the best I have seen in a bilingual government standard. Arabic is treated as an equal language. The rules on confirmation honesty, duplicate payments and recovery states are rigorous. The status vocabulary and the gender-neutral Arabic register solve real problems, and the accessibility statement and exception model are honest. Delivery teams will learn a lot from it.

As an instrument of government, it is not ready. Three problems block adoption:

1. **No authority.** No body has adopted it, enforces it, approves its exceptions or appoints its assessors (OQ-14). Its relationship with the Dubai Digital Authority's own design system and with federal guidance (OQ-7) is open. Without these, any entity can ignore it.
2. **No transition.** It is written for new services built from scratch with `sc-` components. Almost everything it governs is already live, on vendor platforms (a DET React/Next platform, Joomla, Vue and Nuxt, according to the audit) and in Flutter and React Native apps that have no kit. There are no conformance tiers, no deadlines and no legacy route.
3. **An assessment model that cannot run at scale.** Three gates per service, a six-specialist panel with an Arabic content designer and an accessibility specialist on every panel, and panels that run payment-duplication tests themselves. Dubai Now alone describes "over 280 services from 44 entities". This is years of panel time with no funded assessor pool.

Below these, the main risks are:

- The document mixes a service standard with a component library manual. Vendors would be assessed on cubic-bezier curves and KiB budgets.
- In places it legislates where it should defer: mandatory data reuse across entities, the interim emblem rules, and exclusive use of UAE PASS and Dubai Pay.
- Some rules ignore multi-entity reality: a total ban on entity branding, a closed status list that does not fit court procedure, and marketing-led channels.
- It overlooks Dubai's existing mandates, such as the Happiness Meter, the complaints and suggestions route, the Dubai Electronic Security Center baseline and data residency.
- Some items would read badly in public: a motion chapter whose authority is a named individual's GitHub repository, a ban on a sister authority's brand colour, and a date in the future.

**Recommendation.** Do not issue 2.0 as binding. Split the document into three:

- a short **Service Standard** of 12 points with normative MUSTs about outcomes
- a **Design System** reference (tokens, components, motion, budgets), owned by the design system team and outside assessment
- an **Assessment and Assurance handbook** with tiers, a transition plan, procurement clauses and panel guidance

Before any of this is adopted, the issuing authority (OQ-14) and the alignment with Digital Dubai and the UAE Design System (OQ-7) must be settled.

## Top 10 issues

1. **No issuing authority, enforcement lever or legal basis** (0.1, 17.1, OQ-14). Entities, channel owners and vendors have no obligation to comply. Exceptions, disputes and assessor appointments all route to a body that does not exist.
2. **No transition for live services and legacy platforms** (0.2, 2, 10.1, 17.2). The alpha, beta and live gates assume new builds. There is no route for the hundreds of live services, no compliance deadlines, and no tiers such as "must now", "at next major change" and "at re-platforming".
3. **The assessment workload cannot be delivered** (17.2, 17.3). Every service needs three gated assessments, every panel needs mandatory specialists, panels test payments themselves, and every exception is approved centrally and reviewed every month. Without a scaled, risk-based model the standard will stall delivery or be quietly ignored.
4. **The relationship with the Dubai Digital Authority design system, the UAE Design System and TDRA guidance is unresolved, and the teal ban frames it badly** (FND-04, 8 intro, OQ-7). The most likely issuing authority runs the system this one "evolves" and bans by colour. That is a governance collision, not a design detail.
5. **Service standard and component manual are fused** (8, 10, 15). Motion curves, exit percentages, cascade layer order, font subsetting and KiB budgets sit beside legal duties under the same MUST and SHOULD keywords. Assessors' time and vendors' attention go to the wrong things.
6. **It legislates where it should defer to authorities** (principle 6, XCH-03, CHR-02, 4, 14.3). Data must be reused across entity boundaries whatever the legal basis. The emblem interim rules are invented. A law is cited and interpreted while chapter 4 says the standard interprets none. Arabic legal precedence, the Dubai Electronic Security Center baseline, data residency and records retention are missing.
7. **The exclusive identity and payment rules do not match the channels' users** (ID-02, PAY-02). Visitors, overseas investors and foreign logistics firms often do not hold a full UAE PASS. Dubai Trade runs TradePay, and some entities run other approved gateways. A blanket MUST will force every one of these services into an exception.
8. **Multi-entity reality is under-served** (SVC-02, 9.6, CNT-02, CHR-07). The rules ban all entity marks, close the status vocabulary to the 22 words in 9.6 (which does not fit court and customs procedures), and ban marketing language and imagery on channels whose purpose is promotion (Visit Dubai, Invest in Dubai).
9. **The native apps are effectively ungoverned** (7). The highest-volume channel experience is the Dubai Now app. Flutter and React Native have no kits and no generated tokens, and PLAT-06 turns every native team into a standing exception.
10. **No procurement or adoption support** (17.1 "Supplier", 18). There are no contract clauses, acceptance criteria, conformance checklist, training, champions network, shared Arabic content capacity or assessor pool. Vendors cannot price compliance they cannot see in a tender.

## What is strong and should be kept

- **Service behaviour rules that protect the public.** Never show success before the server confirms it (TPL-14, SAVE-02, PAY-04). Duplicate protection on the server (SUB-03, PAY-06). "Checking payment" in place of guessing (PAY-05). Separate "uploaded", "accepted" and "approved" states (UP-03). These prevent the complaints that most damage trust in government services.
- **The separation of "Paid", "Received" and "Approved"** (CNT-19, TPL-16). This is simple, enforceable and directly reduces calls to contact centres.
- **One canonical service record across channels** (SVC-10 to SVC-15, Appendix B). This is the right model for multi-entity, multi-channel government. Owner-confirmed facts, and "not confirmed" when they are not, are excellent governance.
- **Task-led naming** (SVC-04 to SVC-09), with formal names kept as synonyms. Entities can keep their formal names without imposing them on users.
- **Arabic as an equal language in practice:** simultaneous launch (LANG-01), no "translation unavailable" state, bidirectional text isolation, Latin digits with input normalisation, Arabic plural rules, and the gender-neutral register (CNT-11). This is more mature than most regional standards and should become a reference for the UAE.
- **An honest accessibility position:** conformance assessed on whole journeys including UAE PASS and Dubai Pay (A11Y-02). No claim from automated tools or from using components (A11Y-03). An honest statement (A11Y-13). Exceptions that cannot change the truth of the statement (ASR-04).
- **Exception discipline:** expiry dates, severity by effect on people rather than WCAG level (ASR-03), and a staffed alternative for critical services (ASR-05). Keep the principles while scaling the approval route (finding 3).
- **Recovery states for every pattern,** with tests in both languages. This gives assessors concrete things to check and vendors concrete acceptance criteria.
- **Assisted routes and inclusion:** help that does not need speech or written English (HELP-02), staff never asking for credentials (HELP-05), routes for people without an Emirates ID or a UAE phone (ASK-08), and research that includes people of determination, older people, domestic workers and visitors (RES-02).
- **Privacy defaults:** a question protocol (PRV-02), no personal data in URLs (PRV-09), equal-weight cookie choices with nothing non-essential before consent (PRV-12, PRV-13). The audit shows that two channel sites currently offer no reject option, so this fixes a live problem.
- **The deference already built in:** chapter 4 refuses to interpret law, and the open questions list is explicit about what is unconfirmed, with owners. Extend that discipline to the places listed in findings 7 to 11.
- **Requirement identifiers and "How we check" sections.** These make the standard usable in tickets, contracts and reports, and are the foundation for the procurement clauses this review recommends.
